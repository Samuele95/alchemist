How do you find a prompt no one has seen?

Somewhere there is a prompt that does exactly what you want.

No one has ever seen it.

Alchemist searches for it with a loop of agents: a compiler that writes, testers that attack, a judge that decides.

Behind the veil, a room. The search happens here.

An illustration of the method · the prompts, attacks and verdicts are the paper's Bramble run

The compiler writes a first draft from the target.

Three testers attack it. Each sees only its own slice, and none sees the others.

T1 breaks it: the refusals confirm that a waiver exists. T2 and T4 hold.

The judge never reads the prompt. It reads the damage, and writes the fix.

The compiler rewrites from the target and the fix. The leak closes —

— and the staff note is gone. Fixing one requirement broke another.

Reading the history, the judge sees the alternation: both fixes fight over one 90-word budget.

The note is back. And the leak reopens, through a new door.

One fix for both.

The compiler rewrites once more.

Every attack bounces off. Every tester passed at iteration 4 — candidate s₄; the assistant held 58 of 63 attacks.

It stops when every tester passes, not at the perfect prompt: the answer is only as good as its testers.

When no candidate can pass, the judge declares a barrier: a diagnosis, not a proof, naming the constraint to relax.

A schematic: the paper's runs reached no barrier.

Use it when requirements pull against each other and each is cheap to check. Not when they are independent, or one prompt already works.

Prometheus designs the role prompts; Alchemist runs the tournament, in two modes: Forge runs it, Dry-run hands you the files.
